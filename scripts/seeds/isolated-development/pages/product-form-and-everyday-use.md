# Product form and everyday use

When reading about [Steve Jobs](context-use://entity/steve-jobs)’s products, separate what an object looks like, how someone uses it and how it performs commercially. Those are related questions, but one does not answer the others.

The [Power Mac G4 Cube](context-use://entity/power-mac-g4-cube) is recognizable in its [reference photograph](context-use://asset/power-mac-g4-cube-photo); the image was taken later and is not evidence of launch-day demand. [The Cube’s dated trajectory](context-use://page/the-cube-bet) supplies the launch, revised configuration and suspension. Its visual distinctiveness alone cannot explain that outcome.

For [iPod](context-use://entity/ipod), [Jon Rubinstein](context-use://entity/jon-rubinstein)’s [retrospective account](context-use://record/rubinstein-history) attributes industrial design to [Jonathan Ive](context-use://entity/jonathan-ive)’s team and the scroll-wheel suggestion to [Phil Schiller](context-use://entity/phil-schiller). Pair [the product image](context-use://asset/original-ipod-photo) with [the music workflow](context-use://page/what-a-digital-hub-connects) to understand both the object and its use. This is an interpretive reading lens, not a quotation of a design rule Steve stated.
