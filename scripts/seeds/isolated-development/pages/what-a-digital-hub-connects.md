# What a digital hub connects

In [Steve Jobs](context-use://entity/steve-jobs)’s digital-hub proposition, the Mac helps a person work with media and connect it to other devices. This is a guide to that product model; [the digital-hub story](context-use://page/the-mac-as-a-digital-hub) records when its pieces appeared.

[iTunes](context-use://entity/itunes) organizes a music library and playlists. [iPod](context-use://entity/ipod) carries music away from the computer. The [original iTunes record](context-use://record/itunes-launch) describes importing and burning CDs; the [iPod announcement](context-use://record/ipod-launch) describes transferring music through FireWire and synchronizing with iTunes. The software library and the portable player do different jobs inside one activity.

[iMovie](context-use://entity/imovie) and [iDVD](context-use://entity/idvd) provide the parallel route from a home movie to a disc, explored in [the DVD workflow](context-use://page/from-a-home-movie-to-a-dvd). [Mac OS X](context-use://entity/mac-os-x) is the operating-system project, not another media application. Keeping these layers distinct makes Steve’s product story easier to follow without importing later streaming or online-store features into it.
