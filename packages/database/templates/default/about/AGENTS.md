# About conventions

This subtree inherits the [[agents|root guide]]. It adds only conventions for knowledge
whose subject is the owner.

## Entry point and local organization

Use `about/intro` as the concise, instance-owned overview of the owner and the most
useful routes into their knowledge. Its content is not managed by the default template,
and this guide does not prescribe sections for it. Reconcile it as the instance evolves.

Create it on the first write that establishes who the owner is and what they spend their
time on, and reconcile it as that changes. It is the route into everything else, and a
base whose subjects are all reachable except the person they are about has no front door.

The default template supplies three useful subdirectories:

- [[about/diary/agents|Diary]] — chronological lived context and current activity.
- [[about/projects/agents|Projects]] — enduring bodies of work the owner builds or
  stewards.
- [[about/tasks/agents|Tasks]] — finite outcomes, experiments and decision frames.

Other owner-subject knowledge might include a medical record, finances, education, family,
personal history or important documents. These are examples, not prescribed categories.
Preserve the organization already present; when a durable subject genuinely needs a new
home, choose the simplest name and shape that fit this instance. Add a local guide only
when its subtree needs recurring rules.

## What belongs here rather than in a topic

Both this subtree and [[topics/agents|topics]] hold subjects with no beginning or end, so
the question is not how long something lasts. It is whose subject it is.

Ask whether the subject would still exist if the owner did not. Food would. Running,
travel, cooking, a discipline, a field, a practice — each is a thing in the world that the
owner has a relationship with, and that relationship, however personal, is recorded on the
topic. Their cholesterol would not exist without them, nor would their mortgage, their
schooling or their family. Those are the owner's own particulars, true of them and of
nobody else, and they belong here.

One word often covers both, so read the subject rather than the label. *Health* as a
practice — how the owner trains, eats and sleeps — is a topic; *health* as their
conditions, medications and test results is a page here. *Finance* as a field they follow is a topic;
their accounts and obligations are here. Split them when a page tries to be both.

When it is genuinely unclear, prefer the topic. `topics/` is flat, guided and already the
place activity lands when nothing more specific is identifiable, whereas a new subtree here
starts without conventions of its own.

## Choosing diary, project or task

- Use the diary for what happened on a day, what the owner was thinking or feeling, and
  where active work currently stands.
- Use a project for a body of work that breaks down into several finite pursuits.
- Use a task for a finite pursuit, experiment or consequential decision that can resolve
  or close and whose frame remains useful beyond a simple to-do.
- Use a [[topics/agents|topic]] for something with no start and no finish whose subject is
  in the world — a practice, a lens, a field — which lives outside this subtree.
- Use another durable owner page when the subject is the owner themselves: their medical
  record, finances, schooling, family or personal history.

A position the owner reasons from is not one of those pages. An investment thesis, a
standard for stewarding a board, a rule about how they work: each is *about* a subject in
the world, so it belongs on that subject's topic where the decisions citing it can point,
however personal the view is. What stays here is the owner's own particulars, not the
judgements they apply to everything else.

A task may advance a project and should link it; neither should duplicate the other's
account. The relevant child guide supplies local selection and page-shape guidance.
Judgement about another subject stays with that subject under the
[[agents#place-and-identify|root placement rule]].
