# Threads conventions

This subtree inherits the [[agents|root guide]]. A thread is a substantive line of written
correspondence: the asynchronous sibling of a [[meetings/agents|meeting]]. A meeting is one
dated conversation; a thread starts with a message and has no scheduled end.

## Resolve and bound a thread

Apply the root [[agents#identifiability-is-the-threshold|identifiability invariant]] when the
evidence resolves the participants and line of work well enough to distinguish the exchange.
Written correspondence qualifies on the same substantive terms as a spoken conversation.

Bound the knowledge thread by its line of work, not by provider containers. One thread may
continue across email, Slack and forwarded messages; one provider thread may contain two
unrelated knowledge threads. Use the provider boundary when it is already coherent rather
than forcing a clever regrouping.

Name the slug for the subject matter, such as `northwind-renewal-terms`, never for a channel,
counterparty or provider thread id.

Noise excluded by the root curation rule earns no thread. An exchange that only arranges a
meeting belongs to that meeting. One message can establish a thread when its figure,
condition and consequence must be read together; one message carrying one fact is normally a
timeline event on that fact's subject.

## Account and shape

    threads/<thread-slug>/

Every thread begins with both `intro` and `timeline`. This is a local exception to the root
optional-timeline default because progression is the subject.

The thread is the full account of what the exchange established: figures, terms, dates,
conditions, positions, reasons and how they moved. Prefer that useful account over pasted
messages, quote blocks or reply chains.

`intro` says who is involved, since when, what is at stake, where the exchange stands now,
the particulars needed to understand that state, and what remains unresolved. Reconcile it
as positions move. Keep correspondent assertions distinct from the owner's conclusions.

`timeline` records one dated line per material exchange so the sequence shows what was asked,
answered, conceded or changed. New exchanges append in root timeline order; corrections to
existing lines follow the root reconciliation rule. Record what a reply established, not that
a reply arrived.

## Reconcile connected entities

The thread account does not replace its subjects' accounts. In the same coherent write:

- create and link every identified person, organization and other named subject;
- reconcile every durable fact onto the subject that owns it; and
- add a timeline event to an entity when the exchange materially changes that entity.

A particular may correctly appear both in the thread, where its negotiated context matters,
and on the entity, where it happened. The failure is leaving durable knowledge only inside
the exchange.

A thread may reveal a [[about/tasks/agents|task]], [[about/projects/agents|project]] or
[[topics/agents|topic]]. Create and link that subject when its guide resolves it. The thread
keeps what was exchanged; the other page keeps its own question, work or position.

A thread with no material exchange for roughly six months is finished. Reconcile durable
knowledge to its owners and archive the thread.
