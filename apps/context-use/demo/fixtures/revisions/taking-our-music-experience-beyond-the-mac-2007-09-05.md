# Taking our music experience beyond the Mac

The [iPod touch decision](context-use://page/bringing-the-phones-interface-back-to-ipod) closes a loop [I](context-use://entity/steve-jobs) could not see at the [first introduction](context-use://page/putting-the-first-ipod-in-my-pocket). Music led us to a phone; the phone's interface now improves the player.

I reached this point by accepting and then revisiting compromises: [Musicmatch](context-use://page/opening-ipod-to-windows-customers) gave us reach, [Windows iTunes](context-use://page/bringing-our-own-itunes-to-windows) gave us one journey, and [ROKR](context-use://page/comparing-nano-and-rokr-with-tony) showed where a partnership could not resolve the whole interaction. [Shuffle](context-use://page/choosing-a-simpler-player-with-shuffle) and [nano](context-use://page/putting-a-display-into-the-smaller-nano) taught us that smaller products can make different choices about where the work happens.

The family has reached [100 million sales](context-use://page/reaching-one-hundred-million-ipods). I still want the original library-to-pocket test in each review. The [touch launch thread](context-use://record/touch-launch) adds a new shared responsibility: [Scott's](context-use://entity/scott-forstall) application planning must consider both touch and iPhone. The [mini date revision](context-use://page/revising-our-international-mini-schedule) is my reminder to treat the new September availability promise as work to deliver.
