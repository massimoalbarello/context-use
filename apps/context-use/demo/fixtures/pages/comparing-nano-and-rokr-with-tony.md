# Comparing nano and ROKR with Tony

The [September 8 debrief](context-use://record/rokr-debrief), the day after [ROKR shipped](context-use://page/shipping-itunes-on-the-rokr), changed what [I](context-use://entity/steve-jobs) want from the phone work. [Tony](context-use://entity/tony-fadell) could improve a transfer step, but he could not remove all the seams across iTunes, the [ROKR](context-use://entity/motorola-rokr) handset and carrier. The 100-song ceiling also breaks the expectation of carrying a collection.

I had accepted the [Motorola partnership](context-use://page/taking-itunes-onto-motorola-phones) to learn and extend our reach. Comparing it with [nano](context-use://page/putting-a-display-into-the-smaller-nano) made the control problem concrete. The earlier [Windows decision](context-use://page/bringing-our-own-itunes-to-windows) is the precedent: a bridge can be useful without being the final experience.

I asked [Scott](context-use://entity/scott-forstall) for an Apple-controlled phone exploration that includes touch, typing and browsing. [Jony](context-use://entity/jony-ive) will judge the object and interaction together; Tony will preserve the observed failures. This is a direction for the work, not a launch date or a decision to put an iPod wheel on a phone.
