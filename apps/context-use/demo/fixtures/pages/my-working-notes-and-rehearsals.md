# My working notes and rehearsals

[I](context-use://entity/steve-jobs) use rehearsals to find what a polished presentation can conceal. The [first iPod run](context-use://page/walking-the-first-ipod-demo-with-tony) began with an empty player so the transfer was visible. The [Windows run](context-use://page/rehearsing-our-windows-message-with-phil) corrected a misleading claim about what was new. The [Macworld run](context-use://page/walking-the-iphone-demonstration-with-scott) kept the transitions visible because of what we learned from ROKR.

The source material is spread across [a Slack preparation thread](context-use://record/ipod-product-review), [Tony's Granola rehearsal summary](context-use://record/demo-handoff), [Phil's Windows review](context-use://record/windows-handoff) and [Scott's iPhone rehearsal](context-use://record/iphone-rehearsal). I keep the decisions in the linked pages so the next rehearsal can start from the previous lesson.

My recurring checks: start with an ordinary collection, show the handoff, leave time for the unfamiliar action, and separate the introduction from the delivery date. The [crew checklist](context-use://asset/synthetic-ipod-demo-checklist) handles reset details. I should spend my attention on whether the person watching can understand and repeat the useful task.
