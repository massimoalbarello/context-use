# Committing to native applications for iPhone

[I](context-use://entity/steve-jobs) am changing the [web-only launch approach](context-use://page/asking-developers-to-build-for-the-web) and committing to a native SDK for [iPhone](context-use://entity/iphone) and [iPod touch](context-use://entity/ipod-touch) in February 2008. The [October 17 email](context-use://record/native-sdk) makes the promise; it does not announce shipped tools.

The reason sits in the [July Slack follow-up](context-use://record/developer-feedback): offline use, device capabilities and richer interfaces leave concrete gaps. [Scott](context-use://entity/scott-forstall) brought those needs into the [October 16 decision review](context-use://record/native-sdk-preflight). Staying with web applications would preserve the initial boundary but leave useful applications constrained. I chose to open the platform while giving the team time to build a supportable protection model.

This is another turn in the [control question from ROKR](context-use://page/comparing-nano-and-rokr-with-tony). Owning the experience helped us make the phone. It now gives us responsibility for opening it well.

Scott owns scope, developer access and user protection; [Phil](context-use://entity/phil-schiller) owns wording that does not invent distribution details. My next job is to keep scope risks visible and distinguish February's promise from today's decision.
