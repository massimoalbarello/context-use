# Nano and ROKR debrief: a music feature is not a phone experience

Fictional demo · Synced from Granola · 2005-09-08

**Attendees:** Steve Jobs, Tony Fadell, Jony Ive, Scott Forstall\
**Time:** 09:00–09:45 UTC

## What we compared
Find an album, start music, take a call and return to what was playing. Nano makes the music task feel like one product. ROKR exposes boundaries among iTunes, the handset and the carrier; the 100-song ceiling also breaks the expectation of carrying a collection.

## Discussion
Tony argued that another transfer improvement would help the music feature but could not remove every phone-level constraint. Jony asked who could change the whole interaction. Scott proposed reviewing the touch-interface work as a complete device experience.

## Decision
Steve wants an Apple-controlled phone exploration judged on the full sequence. This is a design direction, not a launch date or a decision to reuse the iPod controls unchanged.

## Actions
- Scott: bring a touch interaction review with typing and browsing, not just a music screen.
- Tony: preserve the observed handoff failures for that review.
- Jony: evaluate the object and the interaction together.
- Steve: keep carrier and product responsibilities separate in partner discussions.
